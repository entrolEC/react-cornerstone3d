# The frame is the unit of consistency — one scheduler, no opt-out

Each Binding used to hold its own `requestAnimationFrame`, and the hook exposed `batch: false` to skip the frame and rebuild on every Engine event. That looked like independence but was not: the browser runs rAF callbacks one at a time with a microtask checkpoint between them, and React 19 flushes a `useSyncExternalStore` notification as a SyncLane render in a microtask — so three Bindings dirtied by one scroll of a camera-synced MPR set notified from three callbacks and the component rendered up to three times, and once a second hook family exists (cache and load state), one component could render with hook A on the new frame and hook B on the old one. That is the tearing ADR 0005 chose `useSyncExternalStore` to prevent, reappearing *between* hooks. We decided the frame is the unit of consistency: the library has one scheduler, Bindings only enqueue their rebuild, and a single callback drains the queue so every notification lands in one synchronous pass and React renders once. The scheduler knows nothing about Bindings; the queue of functions is its whole state. We removed `batch: false` in the same decision (a breaking change in 0.x), because within a *state* contract it had no job: intermediate states are not a defect but the definition of a Snapshot (consumers who need events belong on Cornerstone3D's own listeners), a rAF-batched render still lands in the next paint so nothing is visibly late, environments without rAF are a test concern solved by faking frames, and per-event side effects are not React state at all. What it did have was a cost — one unbatched consumer forced every consumer of that viewport sync, and under a shared queue it would have meant jumping the whole library's queue. Rejected alongside: relying on React's automatic batching (it does not span rAF callbacks), coalescing in a microtask instead of a frame (does not span a frame either), and keeping the option with a flush-before-sync rule (complexity in service of a use nobody has). Consequences: a `flush()` for consumer tests is not shipped — fake rAF is the path, and a testing entry point is a separate decision if asked for; a rebuild that throws must not take the rest of the frame down, so the drain isolates each update and rethrows asynchronously.
